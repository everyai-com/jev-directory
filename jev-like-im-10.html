Jev, explained like you're 10

Imagine your school hires a robot to deliver pizza. The robot is fast — but sometimes it forgets to say the price before charging your card. Who checks the robot's work?

Jev is the robot's referee. It watches what the robot did, then answers the coach's questions: did it say the price first? Yes or no. Was it polite? Give it stars. That's the whole job — Jev never delivers pizza itself, it just blows the whistle fairly, every single time.

The three whistles

  1. Yes or no — "Did the robot say $12 before charging?" True or false. No arguing.
  2. Pick one — "Was that a pizza problem, a payment problem, or a lost-driver problem?" One label.
  3. Stars — "How nice was the apology?" One to five stars, like a game review.

Why robots need a referee

Grown-ups let AI robots answer customers, move money, and call tools all day. A human can't re-read ten thousand chats — but Jev can, in seconds, for less than a cent. Every verdict is written down, so when something goes wrong there's a replay tape.
